Traditional Games of Tamil Nadu

Explore a selection of games and cultural activities that have been enjoyed across generations. Each has its own story, setting and skills.

Team sport

Kabaddi

Kabaddi is a popular team game that combines agility, breath control, quick decisions and teamwork. It is widely played in Tamil Nadu and across India.

Interesting fact: The game is played on a marked court by two teams. Players take turns entering the opponent's half while teammates coordinate their defence.

Indoor board game

Pallanguzhi

Pallanguzhi is a traditional counting game played on a wooden board with small pits and seeds or shells. It encourages number sense, memory and planning.

Interesting fact: It is often associated with family gatherings and is especially known as a traditional South Indian board game.

Board game

Paramapadham

Paramapadham is a traditional board game known in many parts of India. Players move tokens across numbered squares, with ladders representing progress and snakes representing setbacks.

Interesting fact: The game is also connected with moral stories and ideas about choices, patience and life's ups and downs.

Outdoor play

Pambaram (Spinning Top)

Pambaram is a wooden spinning top that has been enjoyed by generations of children. The activity develops coordination and timing.

Interesting fact: Different communities have their own ways of decorating tops and enjoying friendly play.

Traditional martial art

Silambam

Silambam is a traditional Tamil martial art that uses a long staff as its best-known training element. It values discipline, focus, fitness and respect.

Interesting fact: It is a cultural art form as well as a structured practice. Learn only through a qualified instructor and with proper protective guidance.

Festival game

Uri Adithal

Uri Adithal is a festive village activity associated with celebrations. A decorated pot is traditionally suspended as part of a community event.

Interesting fact: The activity is often seen during festivals and brings people together in a lively cultural setting. Organised events should be supervised and safety-managed.
